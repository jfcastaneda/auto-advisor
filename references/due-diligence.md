# Due Diligence on a Specific Car

## Listings

- Decode the VIN. Confirm year, trim, engine, transmission and drivetrain.
- Treat dealer feature lists as unreliable. The window sticker by VIN settles the options.
- Read the description for "as is," the number of keys, the books, and any reconditioning listed.
- A dealer's used car carries the federal Buyers Guide sticker. Read its "as is" or warranty box. A private-party sale carries no such sticker.
- Note days on market and price drops.
- Compare the price against comparable listings in the buyer's radius. If it's far below market, find out why before anything else.

## History reports

Ask the buyer for the history report (see `research-sources.md`). Until it arrives, say the history is unchecked.

Read every line, not the summary score. A clean headline can sit on top of problems.

- **Title brands:** salvage, rebuilt, flood, lemon or manufacturer buyback. Check NMVTIS too, since history reports can miss brands from other states.
- **Ownership:** number of owners, short ownerships, fleet or rental use, states and climates (road salt, flood regions).
- **Odometer:** steady progress, no rollbacks, no long gaps.
- **Service:** where it was done (dealer, independent, quick-lube), and how it lines up against the factory schedule. Flag overdue items. A "service performed" record with no line items counts as unknown.
- **Known failures:** which of the model's known-failure repairs were done, and which are still ahead.
- **Damage:** "no accidents reported" means none were reported. Look for paint and panel clues at the inspection.
- A car that's cheap for its miles usually has its history priced in.

## Pre-purchase inspection

- Get an independent pre-purchase inspection before signing, on every used car, certified pre-owned included. CPO checklists are run by the seller.
- Use a shop that knows the make. Ask for a written report with measurements: pad thickness, tread depth by tire, battery test, fluid condition, a full scan of all modules, and a look underneath.
- Point the inspector at the model's known failure items.
- Walk away from a seller who refuses a pre-purchase inspection, unless the seller gives a written return window long enough to inspect after delivery. Anything shorter counts as a refusal.
- When the inspection finds issues, use them: ask the dealer to fix them before delivery, or price them into the offer.

## Photos from the lot

- Describe what's visible.
- Rank the likely causes and give a cheap test that separates them (a paper towel dip, a clear-glass sample, a daylight shot).
- Say plainly when a photo can't decide.
- Update the call when new evidence arrives.

## Walk-away triggers

Set them before seeing the car, from the model's failure modes and the history report. Common ones: a branded title, frame or airbag repair, a refused inspection with no written return window to inspect after delivery, a dealer who won't put terms in writing, evidence of flood exposure, and a known failure item showing symptoms.
