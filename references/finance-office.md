# The Finance Office

Even a cash buyer who negotiated remotely meets the finance manager, who processes the title and contract paperwork. That part is real work. The sales pitches are optional.

## Going in

- Say up front, politely, that all aftermarket products are declined, and ask to sign the declination form.
- Bring the written out-the-door quote. The contract must match it line by line.
- If financing through the dealer, bring the pre-approval and the rate to beat.
- Don't drive the car home until financing is final and in writing. See `situations.md` for spot delivery and yo-yo financing.

## Read the contract

- Selling price, tax rate and amount, title, registration and the doc fee match the quote.
- No new line items. Watch for pre-installed add-ons that appear only now: nitrogen in the tires, VIN etching, paint and fabric protection, anti-theft or tracking devices, "market adjustments."
- For financed deals: the APR, term and amount financed match what was agreed, there's no prepayment penalty, and no products are packed into the amount financed.
- Every blank is filled in or struck out.

## Products, if the buyer wants them

- **Extended warranty:** if the buyer wants one for peace of mind, choose the manufacturer-backed plan over third-party plans. Its price is negotiable, often well below the first quote. Many makers let the buyer buy it later, before the factory warranty ends, from any of their dealers. Check the maker's rules before assuming it.
- **GAP insurance:** worth considering with a small down payment or a long loan. Compare against the buyer's own insurer, which often sells it for less.
- Everything else is declined by default.

## Rules

- Nothing is signed until it matches the quote.
- "It's mandatory" is a claim to check. Pre-installed add-ons can be refused or removed from the price. If the dealer insists, get competing quotes from other dealers first.
- Get copies of everything signed.
