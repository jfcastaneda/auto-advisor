# Locale: Exact Figures (on request)

Use this only when the buyer asks for exact local figures, or is about to sign or check a written quote. The default is the labeled state estimate in `locale.md`.

Keep it proportionate. Go to the official sources first: the state department of revenue for tax, the DMV (or motor vehicle agency) for title, registration and plates, and the state attorney general or consumer protection office for doc fee rules. One good source per fact is enough. Cite each fact with its source and the date checked. List what you couldn't find instead of searching on.

## What to research

**Sales tax**
- The combined rate for the buyer's address: state, county, city and any special district. Look it up by ZIP or address on the state revenue site. Many states tax vehicles at the rate where the buyer registers the car, not where the dealer sits.
- Whether the state has a separate vehicle tax in place of sales tax (some call it a title tax, excise tax or highway use tax).
- What's taxed: the doc fee, dealer add-ons, service contracts, and manufacturer rebates. Some states tax the price before a rebate.
- **Trade-in credit:** whether tax applies only to the price minus the trade-in, and whether that holds for dealer trades only. A few states give no trade-in credit.
- Private-party sales: whether tax is due at registration, and on what value.

**Fees**
- Title fee.
- Registration and plate fees: first year, then the annual renewal. Some states charge by weight, value, age or fuel type. EVs often pay an extra annual fee.
- Plate transfer fee, if the buyer is moving plates from a car they're replacing.
- City or county fees: wheel taxes, city stickers, local registration fees.

**Property or excise tax on vehicles**
- Whether the state, county or city charges a yearly tax on the car's value. Several states do. It belongs in the 5-year cost.

**Dealer rules**
- Doc fee cap. Some states cap it, some require it posted, many don't limit it. Record the cap or "no cap."
- Whether dealers must post or disclose add-ons and fees.

**Used-car law**
- Implied warranty rules and whether dealers can sell "as is."
- Any state used-car warranty or lemon law that covers used cars.
- The federal Buyers Guide sticker is required only on a dealer's used-car sales. Read it on every used car bought from a dealer. A private-party sale carries no such requirement.
- There is no general right to return a car after signing. A return window exists only if the dealer offers it in writing, or where state law grants one, as California's CARS Act now does (see `situations.md`).

**Inspections**
- Safety inspection and emissions testing: whether required, how often, and whether the dealer must pass it before sale.

**Other**
- Any EV or plug-in purchase credits from the state or utility, with their current terms.
- Insurance requirements that change the cost (no-fault states, minimums).
